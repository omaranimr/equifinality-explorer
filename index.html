One hydrograph, many catchments

Streamflow alone accepts catchments that work in very different ways. Add stream δ¹⁸O to the objective function and watch most of them drop out.

Explainer

Definitions

Modelling

Equifinality
Many different models or parameter sets reproduce the observations equally well. A good fit alone does not prove the internal processes are right.
Realization
One parameter set drawn at random from its prior range and run through the model. This tool runs 30,000.
History matching
Keeping only the realizations whose simulations reproduce past observations. Also called calibration.
Objective function J
One score of model–data agreement used to rank realizations. Here a weighted mix of the streamflow fit and the δ¹⁸O fit.
NSE
Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency: 1 minus squared model error divided by the variance of the data. 1 is perfect; 0 is no better than the observed mean.
Accepted (behavioral)
Realizations whose J passes the threshold. All of them are treated as plausible.

Hydrology and tracers

Hydrograph separation
Splitting streamflow into the contributions of different pathways or water sources.
Event and pre-event water
Rain from the current storm versus water already stored in the catchment before it. A tracer can tell them apart; the hydrograph cannot.
Tracer
A measurable property of water, such as δ¹⁸O, that labels where the water came from.
Recession constant k
Response time of a store, from Q = S/k. Small k drains fast; large k sustains slow baseflow.
Runoff coefficient c
Fraction of rain that ends up as streamflow. The rest is lost, mostly to evapotranspiration.
Mixing volume
Stored water that mixes with new arrivals but never drains. It changes the tracer signal, not the streamflow.
Celerity and velocity
How fast a flow response travels versus how fast water molecules travel. Streamflow follows celerity; tracers follow velocity.

Reading the catchment

What is δ¹⁸O?

Each water molecule holds one oxygen atom. Almost all are ¹⁶O; about 2 in 1,000 are the heavier ¹⁸O, with two extra neutrons.

Being about 11% heavier, H₂¹⁸O evaporates less readily and rains out sooner, so each storm and season carries its own fingerprint.

δ¹⁸O = (Rsample ⁄ Rocean − 1) × 1000 ‰
R = ¹⁸O ⁄ ¹⁶O; ocean standard (VSMOW) R ≈ 0.0020052.
There is no δ¹⁶O: ¹⁶O is the denominator.
